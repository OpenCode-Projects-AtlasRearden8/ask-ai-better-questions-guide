# Practice drills

Twelve drills, grouped by the six habits. Work them on paper or in your head — there is nothing to install and no account required. The [answer key](answer-key.md) says which answers earn full credit, why, and what to do on a miss. **You'll get more from doing them badly than from reading them well.**

---

## Drill block A — Adding context (chapter 2)

**A1.** Your friend asks the coffee-shop Wi-Fi chatbot: "Help me write a complaint." Write the two sentences of context you'd have them add *before* the request.

**A2.** A coworker complains: "I asked it to improve my paragraph and it changed everything I liked about it." Diagnose this as a context failure: what single piece of information did the prompt not provide?

**A3.** Rewrite this so it includes the person's experience level: "Explain how a local AI model works."

## Drill block B — Layered follow-ups (chapter 3)

**B1.** You asked for a weekly meal plan for a picky family and the first answer was a wall of text with shrimp tacos twice. Write your (plain-words) follow-up.

**B2.** Which of the six-habits is best described by this message: "Go deeper on point 3, skip the rest" — and what does it prevent?

**B3.** Write the *first* message of a layered conversation for: choosing between a used minivan and a used SUV, given two kids and a 70-mile round-trip commute.

## Drill block C — Critique instead of answer (chapter 4)

**C1.** Rewrite as a critique-first request: "Can you improve my apartment-hunting checklist?"

**C2.** Your resume draft got a total rewrite when you asked for "feedback." What single clause would have prevented that, and what would the model do instead?

**C3.** Name the "reader" for a critique of a birthday card message to your grandmother (who is sharp, hates clutter, doesn't do emojis).

## Drill block D — What am I missing? (chapter 5)

**D1.** You're about to sign a one-year lease on a small warehouse for your eBay overflow inventory. Write your "what am I missing" message — state your plan *including* one assumption the model should challenge (like your rent cap, or your guess about how much space you need).

**D2.** What makes "ask me three questions that would change your answer" different from "what am I missing?" — and when does that difference matter?

**D3.** You said: "I'm pretty sure night shifts are fine for my sleep because I nap before shift." Write the "explain your understanding" version of the *same* message.

## Drill block E — Steering long tasks (chapter 6)

**E1.** You're starting a long task: a moving-and-shipping checklist for relocating your mom's kitchen in one weekend. Name the five things your first message should include (the "five nouns"), briefly, using moving-day words.

**E2.** The model is producing page two of a 5-page document and it's drifting — pages one and two no longer match your original brief. You have one intercept. Write it.

**E3.** Three correction rounds later the chat is tangled. Write the graceful reset, starting with the words "Let's restart cleanly."

## Drill block F — Skepticism (chapter 7)

**F1.** For each answer, mark low/medium/high skepticism and one reason:

(f) "The Empire State Building is 1,454 feet tall, including its antenna."
(g) "Your eBay sales tax rate will be exactly 7.25%."
(h) "Here's a checklist for labeling moving boxes, from 'bedroom' to 'fragile — kitchen.'"

**F2.** The model cited a 2018 Journal of Nursing Studies paper you plan to quote in a staff presentation. First verification move, in ten seconds or less?

**F3.** True or false (and say why): "If the model answers my question three times in a row and gives the same figure each time, that figure is verified."
