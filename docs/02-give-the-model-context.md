# 2. Give the model the context it lacks

## The expert-in-a-telephone-booth model

Here's the correct mental model for every AI chat, stated exactly once, in one sentence: **you are talking to an alarmingly well-read stranger who knows a lot about the world and nothing about you.**

> **Pause and re-read that.** Most bad AI experiences are not a machine failure. They're a briefing failure. The stranger wasn't told enough. The ultimate fix for most disappointing answers isn't smarter phrasing — it's more context.

## What the model doesn't know about you

You are one of millions of people using this tool. From its side, all it has is your words on screen. Anything not on screen does not exist. That includes:

- **Who you are.** Beginner? Years of experience? Twelve-year-old? Nurse? eBay seller of ten years?
- **What you know already.** This is the big one (see the next section of this chapter).
- **What you're trying to do.** The *goal* behind the question. "How do I restart my router" might mean "the internet is down during my kid's homework session" or "I heard it's good hygiene." Same question, different real needs.
- **Your constraints.** Money, time, tools you own, things you refuse to do, deadlines.
- **What "good" looks like to you.** A short list? A step-by-step? Something you can read on your phone while the dog does laps?

None of this is being withheld from you behind a paywall. It's the cheapest upgrade in this entire guide, and it's free: **just say it.**

## Context sentence starters (steal these)

You don't need a form. You need one or two lead-in sentences. Copy any of these:

- "I'm a [who you are] trying to [what you want]."
- "Here's what I currently have: [paste it]."
- "I've already tried [attempt]. That didn't work because [reason]."
- "Explain it as if I've never touched one of these before." (Or the opposite: "Skip the basics, I'm comfortable with the terminology.")
- "I can only use [tool/thing I already own] and I'd like to avoid [thing]."
- "I'm short on time — give me the shortest version that works."

Two of these paste-in sentences routinely double answer quality: naming your experience level, and stating your goal.

## The experience-level fix, in detail

This deserves its own moment, because it's the most common disappointment generator of them all. When you don't say your level, the model aims at a fat middle: it explains "machine learning is a way computers learn from data" when you wanted file structure for a research project, or it rattles off oncology abbreviations when today was your second day on the floor.

- Beginner requests sound like: **"as if I'm brand new, no assumed background, define terms on first use."**
- Advanced requests sound like: **"I already know [X and Y]; go straight to the part about [Z]."**

When you're outside your field, you *are* the beginner — even if you're respected in five other domains. A final exam doesn't care, and neither does the model.

## Partner voice, not telepath voice

One misconception worth killing while we're here: the model does not know you, and it is *trying not to* make assumptions. When it fills a gap, it picks the most statistically common filler in that situation — which is by definition **the norm, not you.**

So when an answer misses, don't conclude "this thing is dumb" or "I'm bad at tech." Diagnose the briefing. Ask yourself three quick questions:

1. Did I say who I am and what I already know?
2. Did I say what I'm actually trying to accomplish?
3. Did I give it anything to work with (an example, a draft, an error message), or did I ask it to perform text-based mind-reading?

Usually one of the three is empty. Fill it, resend, and watch the answer sharpen.

## One small flourish that pays rent

If your answer will be judged by someone else — a hiring manager, a local Buyers Club in your town, a specific format — say that too:

- "This is for a real-estate flyer, so keep it punchy."
- "My manager wants bullets, not paragraphs."

You're not writing a prompt-engineering thesis. You're just a person leaving a better note for a talented-but-clueless temp helper. Notes that say who it's for get better results than notes that don't.

> **The one-line version of this chapter:** *The model can't read your mind, so stop asking it to. Say who you are, what you want, and what you already know.*
